1 · The proposal

One instrument record and one issuer record, read by every book and every control

A wealth manager touches the same security in many places: the brokerage order, the client position, the managed portfolio, the fund's holdings, the price, the concentration limit, the tax form. Each of those systems tends to keep its own copy of the security and its own idea of who the issuer is. The proposal is to publish both once, as two governed data products, and have everything else key on them.

One instrument, many identifiers

The enterprise Instrument ID is stable and never reused. Every CUSIP, ISIN, FIGI, ticker, OCC option symbol and vendor number is a row with a scheme and an effective period, so an identifier change never breaks history.

An issuer is a party

Issuers, guarantors and parents live in the one party master. A bank that is an issuer, a counterparty and a client entity is one party with three roles, so exposure to it can be added up.

Two hierarchies, one stated default

The legal hierarchy says who owns the issuer. The credit parent says who stands behind its debt. Both are kept, and the effective credit parent is the stated default for concentration.

The golden copy explains itself

The model records the rule that ranks sources, the source that won for each instrument and field family, and every approved manual override, so any value can be traced to where it came from.

History is never overwritten

Every table is effective-dated. Matured, delisted and merged instruments are kept, so a holding or trade from any past date still resolves to the instrument and issuer as they were.

Reference once, read everywhere

Holdings, orders, prices, corporate actions, risk and compliance key on the Instrument ID and the issuer's Party ID. No consumer builds its own copy.

3 · Security master

DP-097 Security Master & Identifiers: the instrument, its identifiers, its terms and where each value came from

The golden record of every instrument the firm holds, trades, prices or references as an underlying: equities, bonds, securitized products, third-party funds, own share classes held by own portfolios, listed and OTC derivatives at product level, private assets and limited partner fund interests. It combines the instrument, its identifiers and its listings with the asset-class terms, coupon and redemption schedules, the firm and vendor classifications and the US regulatory eligibility flags (Rule 2a-7, Rule 144A, Regulation S, restricted security, NAIC), each versioned by effective period.

6 · Golden copy

Survivorship is data, not tribal knowledge

A security master can usually tell you a bond's coupon. It often cannot tell you which vendor supplied it, under which rule, or whether someone changed it by hand. The model keeps all three on the record.

Source hierarchy rule

Ranks the licensed datasets for a field family and an instrument scope (asset class, optionally instrument type), with a staleness limit in days after which the next rank is tried.

Field source

Records, per instrument and field family, the dataset whose values won and the rule row it won under, with effective dates.

Attribute override

A single value set by hand: the attribute, the value, the reason, who asked and who approved. It applies only while approved and in force, and the approver must differ from the requester.

Field families. Sources are ranked per family, not per attribute, which keeps the rule set small enough to govern:

Identity Name, type, currency, issue date and identifiersClassification Asset class and vendor sector assignmentsDebt Terms Coupon, maturity, schedules and seniorityEquity Terms Share type, voting, par and shares outstandingStatus Instrument status and listing stateRegulatory Flags Regulatory eligibility classificationsIssuer Link Issuer, guarantor and other party links

Losing vendor values are not stored in the master. Where vendors disagree, the disagreement is a data quality result that the control product reports as a rate.

7 · Issuer hierarchy

DP-098 Issuer Hierarchy, Classifications & Ratings: who issued it, who owns the issuer, who stands behind it

The issuer dimension: every party that issues, guarantees or stands behind an instrument the firm holds or references, with its security-master issuer profile, its legal direct and ultimate parent from the party master, the credit parent overrides research maintains, sector classifications by scheme and agency credit ratings of issuers and issues with history. It takes the party identity, organization profile, legal hierarchy and identifiers from DP-240, so a bank that is both issuer and counterparty is one party.

Two hierarchies and one stated default

The legal hierarchy gives each entity its direct and ultimate parent with the basis, the source and the period, resolved by a firm rule from the parent relationships on record. The credit parent override is for the cases where ownership is the wrong answer to "whose credit is this": an orphan securitization trust, a guaranteed subsidiary, a funding vehicle. The model then states one rule:

Effective credit parent = the approved credit parent override in force; otherwise the legal ultimate parent; otherwise the issuer itself. Credit parent basis records which of the three applied.

8 · Ratings and sectors

Every rating action kept, one composite rule stated

Agency ratings

Each action by each agency is its own row: value, outlook, watch status, action type, date and a sequence that keeps same-day actions apart. Every value maps to a 21-notch scale, so agencies can be compared.

S&P Global RatingsMoody's RatingsFitch RatingsMorningstar DBRSKroll Bond Rating AgencyAM Best
AAA notch 1AA notch 2 to 4A notch 5 to 7BBB notch 8 to 10BB notch 11 to 13B notch 14 to 16CCC and Below notch 17 to 21

Composite per instrument and date: the middle notch where three agencies rate, the lower where two, the single one where one; where the issue is unrated, the issuer's composite. The model publishes the notch, the band, the basis (issue or issuer rating), a split-rated flag and an investment-grade flag.

Sector classification

Instruments and issuers are assigned to a code of a named scheme version, with effective dates, a source and a change reason (first assignment, scheme restructure, corporate event, periodic review, correction). Schemes, their levels, codes and the concordance between schemes are enterprise reference data.

Each issuer has exactly one primary sector scheme at any date, so a sector report never double counts. A manual override of an instrument's vendor code is flagged with its reason.

Schemes that need a license carry a flag, and their codes are served only to consumers entitled to the dataset.

9 · Controls

The record is tested before it is published, and the tests are part of the package

The modeled products carry 23 data quality rules and 58 measures between them. The rules are specific enough to code:

  • An identifier (scheme and value) is active on at most one instrument on any date, and every ISIN and CUSIP passes its check digit.
  • Effective periods never overlap for one instrument and identifier scheme, listing venue, classification scheme or regulatory classification.
  • A merged instrument names its survivor, the survivor is not itself merged, and no identifier stays in force on it after the merge date.
  • Every issuer resolves to an active organization in the party master with an open issuer role.
  • At most one approved credit parent override is in force per issuer, the effective credit parent follows the stated rule, and neither rollup contains a cycle.
  • At most one rating per rated item, agency and rating type is current, and same-day actions are sequenced without gaps.
  • Fields from licensed schemes and datasets reach only entitled consumers.

13 · Path to adoption

Six steps from proposal to the first consumers on the keys

  1. Confirm the record of truthName the security master of record and its vendor feeds, and map their fields to the model's entities. Most of the gaps a mapping finds are identifiers held as columns and terms held as free text.
  2. Write down survivorshipRecord the source ranking per field family and asset class as Instrument Source Hierarchy Rule rows. This is usually tribal knowledge today; as rows it can be reviewed, approved and audited.
  3. Agree the rollupSettle the default concentration rollup with compliance, risk and credit research, and load the credit parent overrides with their approvals.
  4. Conform issuers to the party masterMatch issuers to parties by LEI, then by name and jurisdiction. Decide where new issuer parties are created.
  5. Stand up the two core products with their controlsPublish DP-097 and DP-098 with the data quality rules in this package, and DP-102 so the team can see setups, breaks and overrides from the first day.
  6. Move consumers onto the keysHoldings, orders and pricing first: each keys on the Instrument ID and reads issuer, sector and rating from the issuer product instead of its own copy.
