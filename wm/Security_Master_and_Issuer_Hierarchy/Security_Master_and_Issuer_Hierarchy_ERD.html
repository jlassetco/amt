Security Master and Issuer Hierarchy: product data models

The proposed model, one data product at a time. Each of the 7 products has its own section: what it publishes, drawn as an entity-relationship diagram in crow's foot notation, and what it reads from the other products.

Logical model: attribute names are business names, and no table names, platform data types or indexes are implied. Each box lists the keys and the first 8 other attributes; the workbook and the model map carry every attribute with its definition.

The products

7 data products: two core, three they stand on, two that control them

A data product is the unit that is owned, published and governed. Every entity in the model is published by exactly one product; another product that needs it reads it from there and never keeps a copy. Select a product to go to its model.

An arrow runs from a product to one it reads. Only reads between neighboring tiers and within a tier are drawn; each box lists every product in the package it reads, and each product's section names those outside it.

Diagram 3

The instrument as a supertype and its subtypes

In the diagrams above the instrument is one entity, because each term group has one row per instrument and the proposal folds them into it. This view draws the same 128 attributes the classical way: 22 that every instrument has, and 10 term groups that apply by instrument type.

The subtypes are not mutually exclusive at the second level: a municipal bond carries the debt terms and the municipal terms, a securitized product the debt terms and the securitized terms, and a 529 or ABLE portfolio the fund terms and the municipal fund security terms. Whether these become one wide table, a table per subtype or a table per term group is a physical design choice the logical model leaves open.
